## 1. The business model

NovaCarïs is an AI implementation partner, not a freelance operation. You sell outcomes in small, safe steps, not "AI everywhere."
**Offer ladder:** (1) readiness call, (2) paid assessment, (3) one pilot, (4) staff training, (5) support retainer.
Revenue sits in the assessment, the pilot build and the retainer; training turns the pilot into habit. A productized offer, such as your "Salon Autopilot" package for nail and beauty salons ($799 setup plus $799 per month, as you priced it), shows the pattern: one niche, one outcome, a fixed scope.

## 2. Scaling the training program

Teach once, reuse everywhere: one slide deck, handout and quiz per course (see the Instructor Kits). Train-the-Trainer graduates deliver under your standard once you have observed their teaching. Record sessions only with consent. Plan capacity with a simple formula: *instructor hours = sessions x (session length + prep + grading)*. Add an instructor when one person's weekly hours exceed what they can sustain.

## 4. Niches

Pick one niche before widening, and rank candidates by (a) pain that costs money, (b) your credibility, (c) ability to pay, (d) low regulatory risk for a first pilot. Good candidates: behavioral health agencies, small clinics, nonprofits, salons and other appointment businesses, trades and field services, small law and accounting offices. Test with five conversations before committing; let what people ask for guide you.

## 6. Tool stack

Keep it simple and approved: a general AI assistant with a signed data agreement where private data is involved, a workflow or CRM automation tool for simple automations (for example GoHighLevel for the salon offer), a shared folder, a spreadsheet for tests and logs. Prefer tools the client can keep after you leave. Check each tool's data terms before use.

## 7. Sales scripts (conversation guides, not scripts to memorize)

1. **Cold intro.** Open with their problem, not your tools. "Many [niche] owners tell me [problem]. Is that true for you?"
2. **Discovery call.** Ask what the work looks like step by step; offer the small paid assessment as the next step.
3. **Objection: "Is it safe?"** Answer with your safeguards and an honest limit: AI makes mistakes, so a person reviews what matters.
Never overclaim savings: say "we'll measure it."

## 8. Pricing

Set prices from your cost of time plus the value of the problem. Starting structure: flat fee for the assessment, fixed fee per pilot or setup, monthly retainer for support. *Your numbers, not mine.* Revisit after three clients.

## 9. Autonomous morning market-research agent

An agent scans niche market signals each night and sends a scored morning brief. **It executes nothing without your explicit approval for each item.** This mirrors the human-in-the-loop principle in the AI Governance manual. Add: a named owner, a log of what it read and suggested, a spending limit of zero, and a weekly review of whether its suggestions were useful.

## 10. Legal reminders (for counsel)

Form the business entity (for example an LLC); written contract package (MSA, statement of work, NDA, and a BAA where health information is involved); errors-and-omissions and liability insurance; non-solicit and intellectual-property protection; no guarantees of results; no legal or medical advice; keep client data out of your own tools. This section is a reminder list, not legal advice.

## 11. Calendar setup

A weekly block schedule: selling, delivery, training prep, admin, and protected focus time. Scheduling tool event types (for example a 20-minute intro call, a 60-minute assessment kickoff, a 30-minute check-in). Protect focus blocks so delivery does not get crowded out by calls.

## 12. Open-source and low-cost infrastructure

Where a client cannot afford subscriptions, options include open-weight models (for example Llama), orchestration (LangChain), guardrails (Guardrails AI) and observability (Langfuse), mapped to the visibility ideas in the Governance manual's Chapter 7. Self-hosting shifts security duties onto you or the client: it needs updates, backups and access control. Decision rule: use open source when someone can maintain it; use managed services when the client needs support and a contract.

## 13. Plain-English glossary

*Agent:* AI that takes steps within limits. *BAA:* signed promise to protect patient data. *Baseline:* how things were before. *MSA:* master services agreement. *NDA:* confidentiality agreement. *Pilot:* small first trial. *Retainer:* monthly fee for ongoing support. *SOW:* statement of work, the scope of one project. *Orchestration:* tools that connect AI steps. *Guardrails:* checks that limit what AI may do. *Observability:* seeing what a system is doing. *RAG:* AI that looks up your documents before answering.

## 14. Multilingual training delivery

Prioritize Spanish first, then languages your clients ask for. Use AI for a first-pass translation, then a native speaker reviews, especially compliance-critical content. Keep a translation memory (approved terms), test quizzes in the language used, add subtitles for video, and never rely on machine translation alone for safety or consent text.
