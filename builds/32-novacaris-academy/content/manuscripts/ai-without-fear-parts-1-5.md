## Part 1. What AI is (and is not)

**See it.** Open any AI chat tool and type: "Explain what a library is in two sentences." Read the answer. That is AI at work.

**Understand it.** AI tools like this learn patterns from huge amounts of writing. When you ask something, they predict a helpful answer a small piece of text at a time. They do not "know" things the way a person does, and by default they do not look up a fact. Some tools can also search the web or read documents you give them, which helps but still needs checking.

**What it is good at:** drafting, summarizing, rewording, brainstorming, explaining, organizing.
**What it is not good at on its own:** being sure about facts, making decisions that affect people. Some tools have a "memory" feature that remembers things between chats. Check whether it is on, and what it stores, before you share anything personal.

**Try it.** Ask the same question twice. Notice that the answers differ. That is normal.

**Worked example.** Ask: "Who won the 1987 spelling bee in my town?" A good tool says it cannot know. A careless one may invent a name. This is called a *hallucination*. It is why we check.

**Fear check.** Common fears: "It will take my job" (it changes tasks more than it replaces people who learn to use it); "I'll break it" (you cannot); "It knows everything about me" (only what you type, and only if the tool stores it, so read the privacy settings).

**Words to know:** *AI* (computer systems that perform tasks that seem smart). *Generative AI* (AI that creates new text or images). *Prompt* (your instruction). *Hallucination* (a made-up answer that sounds sure).
**Explain it to a 10-year-old.** AI is a very fast helper that learned from lots of writing. It is great at drafting, but it can be wrong, so we check.

## Part 2. Your first conversation

**See it.** A chat has two sides: you type, it answers. You can reply to improve the answer.

**Understand it.** The AI only sees what is in the chat. If you want it to use a fact, put it in the chat.

**Try it, step by step.**
1. Open an approved AI chat tool.
2. Type: "I'm new to AI. Explain it in five sentences, grade-6 level."
3. Reply: "Now give me three things I can safely try this week."
4. Reply: "Make that a checklist."

**The three-part ask.** *Who I am + what I need + the shape of the answer.*

**Worked example.** Weak: "Help with my resume." Better: "I worked as a home health aide for six years. Turn these duties into five strong resume bullets: [paste]. Keep each under 20 words."

**Safety first.** Do not type passwords, account numbers or private information about other people.

**Words to know:** *Chat* (the back-and-forth with AI). *Context* (what the AI can see in this chat). *Follow-up* (a reply that improves the answer).
**Explain it to a 10-year-old.** You talk, it answers, and you can ask again until it is right. Keep secrets out of the chat.

## Part 3. Good questions get good answers

**See it.** Two prompts, side by side: "Write a flyer" and a detailed one. The second wins.

**Understand it.** The AI fills gaps with guesses. Fewer gaps, fewer wrong guesses.

**The eight habits** (see *Prompt Engineering for Beginners*): say who you are; give background; one task; show an example; set the shape; let it ask you; check; improve in steps.

**Try it.** Take something you need this week (an email, a menu, a schedule). Write a prompt using at least four habits. Compare with what you would have typed before.

**Worked example.** "Plan a food drive" becomes "I'm organizing a one-day food drive for 40 volunteers at our church. Make a checklist for the week before, with who does what. Ask me three questions first."

**Words to know:** *Role* (who you ask AI to act as). *Format* (shape of the answer). *Example* (a sample you want it to match).
**Explain it to a 10-year-old.** The clearer your question, the better the answer. Give details, like ordering a sandwich exactly how you like it.

## Part 4. Check, protect, and stay in charge

**See it.** An answer with a made-up statistic, circled in red.

**Understand it.** You are the boss; the AI is the assistant. You are responsible for what you send.

**The Check Routine (60 seconds).**
1. Names, dates, numbers: are they right?
2. Does it say anything I cannot prove?
3. Would I be comfortable if my boss read this?
4. Did I include anything private?

**The stoplight.**
- **GO:** public information, in an approved tool.
- **CHECK:** internal information; ask your owner first.
- **STOP:** private or patient information in an unapproved tool.

**Try it.** Ask AI for three facts about your city. Verify each with an official source. Note which, if any, were wrong.

**Worked example.** An assistant drafts a note with a medicine the client does not take. The signer reads closely, finds it and fixes it. Human review worked.

**Words to know:** *Verify* (confirm with a reliable source). *Privacy* (keeping personal information safe). *Human in the loop* (a person approves before action).
**Explain it to a 10-year-old.** Always double-check the helper's work, and never tell it secrets.

## Part 5. Making AI do real work

**See it.** A before-and-after: a messy meeting note turned into a clean action list.

**Understand it.** Most everyday value comes from five jobs: *summarize*, *rewrite*, *organize*, *brainstorm*, *explain*.

**Try it: five small projects (15 minutes each).**
1. **Summarize:** paste a public article; ask for five bullets.
2. **Rewrite:** make a rough message friendlier and shorter.
3. **Organize:** turn a list of tasks into a weekly schedule.
4. **Brainstorm:** ask for ten names or ideas, then pick two.
5. **Explain:** ask it to explain a confusing form in plain words, then check against the form.

**Build a personal prompt library.** Save your best prompts in a document with a title and a one-line note about when to use each.

**Worked example.** A nonprofit coordinator keeps a "Monday email" prompt: she pastes five bullet points and receives a friendly staff update. Saves her thirty minutes a week, and she still reads it before sending.

**What comes next.** Part 6 begins building small tools and automations. Before that, finish the checklist below.

**Part 5 checklist**
- [ ] I can write a clear prompt using four habits.
- [ ] I check every answer before using it.
- [ ] I know what never goes into an unapproved tool.
- [ ] I have three saved prompts.

**Words to know:** *Summarize* (shorten while keeping the main points). *Prompt library* (your saved prompts). *Workflow* (steps you repeat).
**Explain it to a 10-year-old.** Once you know how to ask and how to check, AI can help with boring chores like summarizing and organizing, and you stay the boss.
