## L01 · AI Is Not Magic

**Hook.** A friend shows you a phone that writes a poem in four seconds. You think, "That must be magic" or "I could never understand that." Both thoughts stop you from learning.

**In plain words.** AI is a tool built from parts: information it learned from, a model, instructions you give it, and sometimes tools it can use. Open the box and the mystery shrinks.

**Under the hood.** DATA → MODEL → INSTRUCTIONS → TOOLS → OUTPUT. Each piece can be inspected, tested and improved by people.

**Visual.** Split-scene illustration: on the left, a glowing mysterious AI orb surrounded by question marks; on the right, the same orb opened into understandable layers labeled DATA → MODEL → INSTRUCTIONS → TOOLS → OUTPUT.

**Today's AI Word — Artificial Intelligence.** Computer systems designed to perform tasks that normally require human-like abilities such as language understanding, pattern recognition, prediction or decision support. *Remember: AI is a capability, not a single product.*

**Your Turn (5–10 min).** Ask an AI: "Explain artificial intelligence to me like I am 12 years old, using a restaurant as the example." Then ask it to explain the same idea to a business owner.

**AI got it wrong.** Ask an AI, "Is AI alive?" Some answers sound mysterious or human. Notice that a confident, friendly voice is not proof of understanding.

**FDE question.** A business owner says, "I need AI." What is the first question you should ask before recommending a technology? *(Model answer: What problem are you trying to solve, and how will we know it is solved?)*

**Human in the loop.** The learner, not the AI, decides what counts as a useful answer.

**Check yourself.** (1) Name the five layers of the AI box. *(Data, model, instructions, tools, output.)* (2) Is AI one product or a capability? *(A capability.)*

**You can now:** explain that AI is built from parts you can understand.
**Words to know:** *AI* (computer systems doing tasks that seem smart). *Model* (the trained engine). *Output* (what the AI gives back).

## L02 · What AI Actually Does

**Hook.** You drop a messy email, a PDF and a photo of a receipt into a tool. Out come a summary, a list of facts and a draft reply. What just happened?

**In plain words.** AI takes something in, finds patterns, and gives something out. **INPUT → AI SYSTEM → OUTPUT.**

**Under the hood.** Inputs can be text, images, audio, numbers or documents. Outputs can be answers, summaries, categories, predictions, drafts, code or actions. The quality of the input shapes the quality of the output.

**Visual.** A bright factory conveyor shows an email, PDF, photo and spoken request entering a machine; different outputs emerge: summary, answer, extracted data, recommendation and draft response.

**Today's AI Word — Input.** Information provided to a system. *Remember: if the input is poor, the output may be poor.*

**Your Turn.** Give an AI the same piece of information three ways: paste the text, upload the document, and describe it yourself. Compare the outputs.

**AI got it wrong.** Paste a blurry, half-copied table and ask for totals. The AI may "fill in" numbers that were never there. Bad input produced a confident but wrong output.

**FDE question.** If a customer complains that their AI answers are unreliable, what would you inspect before blaming the model? *(The inputs: missing documents, messy data, unclear instructions.)*

**Human in the loop.** A person checks that the input is complete and appropriate to share before it goes in. Private data never goes into an unapproved tool.

**Check yourself.** (1) Write the three-box model. *(Input → AI system → output.)* (2) Why can a good model give a bad answer? *(Poor input.)*

**You can now:** describe what goes into and comes out of an AI system.
**Words to know:** *Input* (what you give it). *Pattern* (something that repeats and can be recognized). *Summary* (a shorter version with the key points).

## L03 · AI vs Automation vs Software

**Hook.** A web form emails you every time someone signs up. Is that AI? No. Then what is it?

**In plain words.** **Software** follows programmed rules. **Automation** moves a repeatable job along by itself. **AI** can interpret, generate or predict. Real solutions often combine all three.

**Under the hood.** Software stores the inquiry. Automation routes it to the right person. AI reads the message, labels the request and drafts a reply. A human approves anything sensitive.

**Visual.** A small business receives an online inquiry. Software stores it; automation routes it; AI understands the message, classifies the request and drafts a response; a human approves sensitive actions.

**Today's AI Word — Automation.** A system that performs a repeatable task or workflow automatically. *Remember: automation answers "what should happen every time?"*

**Your Turn.** Describe one repetitive task in your life or workplace. Write the steps. Circle the steps that require judgment and underline the steps that are purely repetitive.

**AI got it wrong.** Use AI to do something a simple rule does perfectly, like "send every invoice over $500 to the manager." The AI may occasionally miss it. When a rule is enough, use the rule.

**FDE question.** Which parts of a customer's process need AI, and which just need software or automation? *(Judgment and language: AI. Repeatable steps: automation. Storage and rules: software.)*

**Human in the loop.** A person decides which steps may run without review.

**Check yourself.** (1) Which one answers "what happens every time?" *(Automation.)* (2) Give a task that needs judgment. *(Example: deciding how to word a sensitive reply.)*

**You can now:** sort a task into software, automation or AI.
**Words to know:** *Software* (programs that follow rules). *Automation* (steps that run on their own). *Workflow* (the steps of a job).

## L04 · What Is an LLM?

**Hook.** You ask a chat tool to explain a contract clause and it writes three clear paragraphs. What is doing the writing?

**In plain words.** LLM stands for **Large Language Model**: a language engine trained on enormous amounts of text so it can recognize patterns in language and write useful text. It is not a human brain. It does not automatically know what is true, and it does not know your private business information unless you give it.

**Under the hood.** An LLM produces text from three things: patterns learned in training, the current context, and your instructions. It generates text a small piece at a time, choosing likely next pieces. That is why it sounds fluent, and also why it can be fluent and wrong.

**Visual.** A transparent futuristic engine has three visible inputs (training patterns, current context and instructions) and produces text, code, summaries and structured answers.

**Today's AI Word — LLM.** Large Language Model: a type of AI model designed to work with language. *Remember: LLM is the engine; an AI application is the system built around the engine.*

**Your Turn.** Ask an AI: "Give me five things an LLM is good at and five things it should not be trusted to do without verification."

**AI got it wrong.** Ask an AI a very specific fact you know well. Compare its answer with your trusted source. The goal is to notice that confidence and correctness are different.

**FDE question.** A customer thinks the chat tool "knows" their company policies. How do you explain what it actually has access to? *(Only training patterns and what you supply in this conversation or connect to it.)*

**Human in the loop.** A person verifies facts that matter before anyone acts on them.

**Check yourself.** (1) What does LLM stand for? (2) Is an LLM the same as an app like a chat tool? *(No. The LLM is the engine; the app is the whole system.)*

**You can now:** explain an LLM without calling it a brain.
**Words to know:** *LLM* (language engine). *Training* (learning patterns from lots of text). *Application* (the product built around a model).

## L05 · What Is a Prompt?

**Hook.** "Write an email" gets you something bland. A clearer ask gets something you can send. The difference is not a secret phrase.

**In plain words.** A prompt is the instruction or information you give an AI. Good prompting is clear communication: **GOAL + CONTEXT + RULES + OUTPUT = STRONGER PROMPT.** *Goal:* what you want done. *Context:* what it needs to know. *Rules:* what to do or avoid. *Output:* what the answer should look like.

**Under the hood.** The model reads your whole prompt as input. Ambiguity gets filled with guesses. Fewer gaps, fewer wrong guesses.

**Visual.** Two prompt cards side by side: "Write an email" versus a structured prompt showing goal, audience, tone, facts, constraints and desired format.

**Your Turn.** Start with "Write a customer email." Then rewrite it using Goal + Context + Rules + Output. Compare the results.

**Today's AI Word — Context.** The information available to the AI for the current task. *Remember: AI can only use the context it has access to.*

**AI got it wrong.** A prompt with conflicting rules ("be very short" and "explain in detail") produces a muddled answer. Fix the prompt, not the AI.

**FDE question.** A customer says, "The AI is bad at following instructions." What questions would you ask about the prompt before changing the model? *(Is the goal clear? Is any needed context missing? Do the rules conflict? Is the output format stated?)*

**Human in the loop.** A person reads the result before sending it to anyone.

**Check yourself.** (1) List the four parts of a stronger prompt. (2) Is there a magic phrase? *(No.)*

**You can now:** turn a vague request into a clear one.
**Words to know:** *Prompt* (your instruction). *Context* (information the AI can use now). *Constraint* (a rule or limit).

## L06 · Context: What AI Knows Right Now

**Hook.** You hire a brilliant consultant and refuse to show them the customer file. Their answer will be limited.

**In plain words.** An AI response depends heavily on the information it can see at that moment. Give it the policy, the notes and the facts, and the answer improves.

**Under the hood.** The **context window** is how much information a model can consider in one interaction. More is not automatically better. Relevant is better. Very long, cluttered context can bury what matters.

**Visual.** A capable consultant stands in two rooms: one empty except for a question, and one filled with current customer documents, policies and data. The second room produces a much more grounded answer.

**Today's AI Word — Context Window.** The amount of information a model can consider in a particular interaction. *Remember: more context is not automatically better; useful, relevant context is better.*

**Your Turn.** Give an AI a short policy document and ask it to answer five questions using only that document. Then ask a question not answered by the document. Notice what happens.

**AI got it wrong.** If the AI answers a question that the supplied document never addressed, treat that as a signal to verify, not as proof the AI knows the answer.

**FDE question.** A customer's assistant gives outdated answers. Where would you look first? *(What information it is being given, and whether it is current.)*

**Human in the loop.** A person decides which documents are approved to share with the tool.

**Check yourself.** (1) What is a context window? (2) Why not paste everything? *(Irrelevant text can hide what matters and may expose private data.)*

**You can now:** supply the right information for a task.
**Words to know:** *Context window* (how much it can consider at once). *Grounded* (based on supplied facts). *Relevant* (related to the question).

## L07 · AI Can Be Brilliant—and Wrong

**Hook.** A beautifully written answer cites a study that does not exist. It looked perfect.

**In plain words.** This is one of the most important lessons in the book. AI can produce a polished answer that is wrong. The professional response is not fear. It is evaluation: **GENERATE → CHECK → CORRECT → IMPROVE.**

**Under the hood.** Because an LLM predicts likely text, it can produce plausible-sounding statements with no source behind them. Some tools add web search or document lookup, which helps but does not remove the need to check.

**Visual.** A polished AI answer appears on a screen with a large verification warning; beside it, a human checks a primary source and corrects the result.

**Today's AI Word — Hallucination.** When an AI generates information that is unsupported, fabricated or incorrect while presenting it as an answer. *Remember: fluent does not mean factual.*

**Your Turn.** Ask an AI for three facts about a topic you know. Verify every fact. Mark each as VERIFIED, UNCERTAIN or WRONG.

**AI got it wrong.** Ask for "three published sources with links" on a narrow topic. Click every link. Some may not exist or may not say what the AI claims.

**FDE question.** You are deploying an AI assistant for a customer. What safeguards would you put around high-impact answers? *(Source citations, human review, a "don't know" path, testing, monitoring.)*

**Human in the loop.** Anything that affects health, money, jobs or legal rights gets a person's review before use.

**Check yourself.** (1) Define hallucination. (2) Give the four-step habit. *(Generate, check, correct, improve.)*

**You can now:** check an AI answer instead of trusting it blindly.
**Words to know:** *Hallucination* (made-up answer that sounds sure). *Verify* (confirm with a reliable source). *Primary source* (the original, trusted record).

## L08 · Your First Useful AI Task

**Hook.** Enough talk. Pick one small thing you already do and let AI help.

**In plain words.** Choose a task that is useful, repeatable and low-risk: summarize a meeting, turn notes into a checklist, draft a customer email, organize a messy list, compare two documents, make a study plan, or explain a hard idea. Do not start with a mission-critical decision.

**Under the hood.** You are building a tiny workflow: task → context → prompt → output → review → improved prompt.

**Visual.** A desk covered with notes, emails and documents flows into an AI workspace and becomes a clean checklist, summary and action plan, with a human reviewing the final result.

**Today's AI Word — Workflow.** A sequence of steps used to complete a task. *Remember: AI becomes more valuable when it is placed inside a useful workflow.*

**Your Turn.** Write the task in one sentence. Give the AI the necessary context. Ask for a specific output. Review it. Improve your prompt once. Save both versions.

**Build It.** Save your best prompt as a reusable template with blanks for the changing details.

**AI got it wrong.** Ask the AI to summarize notes that contain a date. Check the date in the summary against your notes. Small details are where errors hide.

**Human in the loop.** You read the result and decide whether to use it.

**Check yourself.** (1) What makes a good first task? *(Useful, repeatable, low-risk.)* (2) Why save both prompt versions? *(To see what improved.)*

**You can now:** complete one real task with AI and keep the prompt for next time.
**Words to know:** *Workflow* (steps of a job). *Template* (a reusable pattern with blanks). *Low-risk* (little harm if it is wrong).

## L09 · The Human Is Still in the Loop

**Hook.** An AI auto-replies to every customer overnight. One reply promises a refund the company never offers.

**In plain words.** Good AI systems do not always remove people. Often they make people faster and better. For low-risk tasks, AI may act automatically. For high-impact tasks, a person reviews, approves or overrides.

**Under the hood.** Design the checkpoints on purpose: AUTO (safe to run), REVIEW (a person checks), HUMAN-ONLY (a person decides).

**Visual.** An AI system handles routine work automatically, but important decisions pause at a clear human approval checkpoint before an action is taken.

**Today's AI Word — Human-in-the-Loop.** A design in which a person reviews, approves, corrects or supervises an AI system at important points. *Remember: the right question is not "Can AI do it?" but "What should AI do, and where should a human remain responsible?"*

**Your Turn.** Take the task from Lesson 8. Mark each step AUTO, REVIEW or HUMAN-ONLY. Explain why.

**AI got it wrong.** Imagine the refund example above. Which step should have been REVIEW?

**Human in the loop.** *(This whole lesson.)* Name the person who is accountable for each step.

**Check yourself.** (1) What do AUTO, REVIEW and HUMAN-ONLY mean? (2) Who is responsible for what the AI sends? *(A person.)*

**You can now:** place human checkpoints where mistakes would matter.
**Words to know:** *Human-in-the-loop* (a person supervises at key points). *Approval* (a person's yes before action). *Accountable* (answerable for the result).

## L10 · Your First AI Mental Model

**Hook.** You now know enough words to draw the whole picture on one page.

**In plain words.** Everything so far fits in one map: **USER → GOAL + CONTEXT + INSTRUCTIONS → AI MODEL + TOOLS + DATA → OUTPUT / ACTION → EVALUATION + HUMAN OVERSIGHT.**

**Under the hood.** Each box is a place where quality can be improved or things can go wrong. When results disappoint, ask which box is the problem.

**Visual.** A clean futuristic system map connecting a human, goal, context, model, tools, data, output and evaluation loop. Each component is visually distinct and connected by glowing lines.

**Today's AI Word — Evaluation.** A structured way to test whether an AI system is performing well enough for its intended purpose. *Remember: if you cannot measure whether the system works, you cannot confidently improve it.*

**Your Turn.** Draw the map from memory, then label where a human checks the work.

**AI got it wrong.** Pick a bad AI answer you have seen. Point to the box on the map where it likely went wrong.

**FDE question.** A customer gives you a vague request: "Build us an AI assistant." Use the mental model to list the information you would need before building. *(Who the user is, the goal, available context and data, rules, tools, what good looks like, and who oversees it.)*

**Human in the loop.** The map always ends with a person.

**Check yourself.** See the Part 1 checkpoint below.

**You can now:** explain an AI system in one minute.
**Words to know:** *Mental model* (a simple picture in your head). *System* (parts that work together). *Evaluation* (testing whether it works).
