# Prompt Engineering for Beginners

**A 15-minute quick reference: eight habits for getting useful answers from AI**

## How to use this guide

Read it once (15 minutes). Then keep the one-page card at the end next to your screen. A *prompt* is simply what you type to AI. A better prompt gives a better first answer and saves you time.

**Explain it to a 10-year-old.** Asking AI is like asking a very fast helper who knows nothing about your day. The more clearly you explain the job, the better the help.

## The eight habits

### 1. Say who you are and what you need
Give the helper a role and a goal.
- *Weak:* "Write an email."
- *Better:* "I run a small home-care agency. Write a friendly email to families explaining our new visit schedule."

### 2. Give the background
The AI only knows what you tell it. Add who the audience is, what happened and what matters.
- *Better:* "The families are mostly older adults. The change starts Monday. Visits move from mornings to afternoons."

### 3. Ask for one clear task
One job per request beats five jumbled ones.
- *Weak:* "Fix this, shorten it, translate it and make a poster."
- *Better:* "Shorten this to three sentences." Then the next step.

### 4. Show an example
Paste a sample of the style or format you like.
- "Here is a past email I liked: [paste]. Match this tone."

### 5. Set the shape of the answer
Say the format, length and reading level.
- "Give me a table with three columns." "Under 150 words." "Reading level: grade 6."

### 6. Ask it to ask you
If the request is big, let the AI collect what it needs.
- "Before you start, ask me up to five questions you need answered."

### 7. Check the answer
AI can sound sure and be wrong. Check names, numbers, dates and anything that could hurt someone.
- Ask: "List the claims here I should verify." Then verify them yourself.

### 8. Improve in steps
The first answer is a draft. Say what to change.
- "Warmer tone." "Cut the second paragraph." "Add a one-line summary at the top."

## Protect private information (the ninth rule that overrides the others)

Do not type private details (names with health information, passwords, account numbers) into any tool your organization has not approved. Use the **stoplight**: *Go* public information; *Check* internal information, ask first; *Stop* private data in an unapproved tool.

## Worked example: from weak to strong

**Weak prompt:** "Write a flyer."

**Strong prompt:** "I'm the director of a small community training center. Write a one-page flyer for a free two-hour evening class called 'AI Without Fear' for adults with no technical background. Friendly, plain words, grade-6 reading level. Include what they'll learn (three bullets), the date [fill in], location [fill in] and a sign-up line. Ask me for any missing detail before you write."

*Why it works:* role, background, one task, format, reading level, and permission to ask questions.

## Fill-in-the-blanks template

> I am [who you are]. I need [one task]. The audience is [who]. Background: [facts]. Format: [shape and length]. Tone: [tone]. Here is an example I like: [sample]. Ask me questions first if anything is unclear.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| Too vague | Add role, audience, format |
| Too many tasks at once | One task per message |
| Trusting every answer | Check facts and numbers |
| Pasting private data | Stoplight rule |
| Giving up after one try | Improve in steps |

## One-page card

1. Who I am and what I need
2. Background
3. One task
4. Example
5. Shape of the answer
6. Let it ask me questions
7. Check it
8. Improve in steps
**Plus:** protect private information.

**Words to know:** *Prompt* (what you type). *Role* (who you ask the AI to act as). *Format* (the shape of the answer). *Hallucination* (a confident answer that is made up).
