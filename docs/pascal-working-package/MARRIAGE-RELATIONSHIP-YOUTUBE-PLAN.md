# Faceless YouTube — Marriage & Relationships

## Channel promise
Help people understand difficult relationship patterns and learn practical ways to build healthier marriages.

## Evidence discipline
The supplied notes describe relationship advice as an established content category and identify Google Trends and YouTube Analytics as useful research tools. Those signals do not establish that a specific proposed title has high demand. The exact rankings and content gaps below are hypotheses until validated against current search results and channel analytics.

## 20 video ideas
1. **Why Your Spouse Shuts Down During Arguments** — Title: “Why Your Spouse Shuts Down During Arguments—and What to Do.” Hook: “You ask a simple question, and suddenly your spouse goes silent. What might be happening?” Gap to investigate: practical words to say, a safe pause, and a way to return to the conversation.
2. **Feeling Lonely Inside Your Marriage** — “Married but Lonely? 7 Ways Couples Become Emotionally Distant.” Hook: “You share a home and daily responsibilities, but still feel alone. How does that happen?” Investigate whether practical reconnection exercises are missing.
3. **Difficult Season or Unhealthy Pattern?** — “A Rough Patch or an Unhealthy Marriage? How to Tell the Difference.” Hook: “When does a temporary problem become a pattern that needs serious attention?” Investigate balanced treatment of stress, disrespect, accountability, and support.
4. **Disagree Without Damaging the Relationship** — “How to Fight Fair in Marriage: 5 Rules That Change the Conversation.” Hook: “The goal isn't to defeat your spouse; it's to solve a problem without damaging the relationship.” Investigate realistic dialogue and repair examples.
5. **Rebuilding Trust After Betrayal** — “How to Rebuild Trust After Betrayal: What Must Change First?” Hook: “An apology can start a conversation, but what helps rebuild trust?” Investigate accountability and consistent behavior.
6. **Unequal Effort / Emotional Labor** — “Are You Carrying Your Marriage Alone? 6 Patterns to Recognize.” Hook: “If you initiate every conversation and repair, resentment can grow.” Investigate without assuming one partner's view is the whole story.
7. **Boundaries vs. Control** — “Healthy Boundary or Controlling Behavior? Learn the Difference.” Hook: “A boundary explains what you will do; control tries to dictate what another person may do.” Investigate everyday examples.
8. **Apologies That Help** — “Why ‘I'm Sorry’ Sometimes Isn't Enough in Marriage.” Hook: “You apologized, but your partner is still hurt. What might be missing?” Investigate weak versus accountable apologies.
9. **When Affection Disappears** — “Why Has Affection Faded in Your Marriage? 6 Things to Explore.” Hook: “When warmth and small gestures disappear, couples can feel like roommates.” Investigate stress, health, distance, and differing needs without assuming infidelity.
10. **Money Arguments** — “Why Couples Fight About Money—and How to Talk About It.” Hook: “The argument may be about more than the numbers.” Investigate a joint money conversation and simple budget exercise.
11. **Starting a Difficult Conversation** — “How to Talk About a Problem Without Starting a Fight.” Hook: “You need to bring it up, but remember how the last conversation ended.” Investigate opening scripts and pause plans.
12. **Avoiding Future Conversations** — “Your Spouse Avoids Talking About the Future—What Now?” Hook: “You want to discuss plans or commitment, but the conversation keeps disappearing.” Investigate without guessing hidden motives.
13. **Small Connection Habits** — “7 Small Daily Habits That Help Couples Stay Connected.” Hook: “Connection is often built through small moments repeated over time.” Investigate realistic low-cost habits.
14. **Repeating the Same Argument** — “Same Fight, Different Day? Here's How to Break the Cycle.” Hook: “The topic changes, but the argument ends the same way.” Investigate triggers, reactions, escalation, and repair using a fictional couple.
15. **Quiet Resentment** — “7 Signs Resentment Is Growing in Your Marriage.” Hook: “Resentment may look like indifference, sarcasm, or giving up asking.” Avoid presenting signs as diagnoses.
16. **Emotional Safety** — “What Does Emotional Safety in Marriage Actually Look Like?” Hook: “Can you disagree or admit a mistake without fearing humiliation or retaliation?” Investigate observable behaviors.
17. **Reconnecting After Parenthood** — “After the Kids, Why Does Your Marriage Feel Different?” Hook: “When parenting takes over every conversation, connection can get crowded out.” Investigate short practices for busy parents.
18. **In-Laws and Family Boundaries** — “When In-Laws Get Involved: How Couples Can Set Respectful Boundaries.” Hook: “How do you protect your marriage without escalating every family disagreement?” Investigate culturally respectful scripts.
19. **When to Seek Professional Help** — “Marriage Counseling: When Is It Time to Get Help?” Hook: “You don't have to wait until a relationship reaches a breaking point to seek support.” Investigate benefits, limits, and situations where joint counseling may be unsafe.
20. **Considering Leaving** — “Should You Stay or Leave Your Marriage? Questions to Consider.” Hook: “When you're exhausted and uncertain, pressure to decide quickly may not help.” Offer balanced reflection and prioritize safety where abuse is present.

## Research checklist for the first five
- [ ] Search each exact topic on YouTube and log the first 10 relevant videos.
- [ ] Record title, upload date, views, channel subscribers when visible, and video length.
- [ ] Compare views with each channel's usual performance; don't rely on raw totals alone.
- [ ] Read recent comments for repeated questions and requests.
- [ ] Compare related terms in Google Trends (US, last 12 months, YouTube Search where available).
- [ ] Check YouTube Studio → Analytics → Trends for available search and content-gap signals.
- [ ] State one specific gap that the video will address.
- [ ] Match title and thumbnail promises to the finished content.

## Gaps to investigate, not assume
- Exact scripts for difficult conversations.
- Clear distinction between similar situations (e.g., boundary versus control).
- Practical next steps rather than lists of symptoms.
- Fair treatment of both partners' perspectives.
- Original fictional scenarios, simple diagrams, and narrated examples.

## 30-day test
- **Days 1–3:** research five leading topics and choose the first two based on evidence.
- **Days 4–10:** produce and publish two original faceless videos.
- **Days 11–17:** publish two more videos with different problems/hooks.
- **Days 18–24:** publish the fifth video and make related Shorts from strong material.
- **Days 25–30:** compare analytics and comments; select the next topic cluster.

Track impressions, click-through rate, first-30-second retention, average view duration, traffic sources, comments, and subscribers gained. Compare videos of similar length and account for video age.

## Safety note
Do not diagnose a spouse or present videos as a substitute for professional help. For abuse, coercive control, or fear for safety, prioritize safety resources and do not assume joint communication exercises are appropriate.
